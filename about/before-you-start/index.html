    Before You Start

    Before diving into code and tutorials, this page gives you a quick overview of what AirDB expects, the real-world characteristics of Bluetooth Low Energy, and the mental models that will help you design reliable off-grid applications.

    Who This Documentation Is For

    AirDB is designed for: - Android Developers building apps in Kotlin with Jetpack Room, SQLite, or SQLDelight. - Apple Developers building apps for iOS, iPadOS, macOS, or watchOS using Swift, SwiftData, CoreData, or GRDB. - Distributed Systems Engineers looking for an off-grid replication protocol that guarantees convergence without relying on cloud coordination.

    You do not need a background in advanced cryptography or distributed math to use AirDB. The high-level APIs handle packet serialization, encryption, and peer discovery out of the box.

    Important Mental Models

    1. Bluetooth Low Energy Is Not Wi-Fi

    BLE is designed for power efficiency, not raw file downloading: - Bandwidth: Effective throughput over BLE typically ranges from 20 KB/s to 80 KB/s, depending on physical distance, radio interference, and device chipset capabilities. - Packet Size (MTU): Raw BLE packets are small (typically between 23 and 512 bytes). AirDB automatically handles payload compression and splits larger packets into smaller chunks for you. - What to sync: AirDB is designed for structured data (such as survivor triage records, inventory changes, sensor logs, and JSON or Protobuf payloads). For large multi-megabyte photo or video attachments, the recommended pattern is to replicate the file metadata via AirDB, and trigger a direct high-speed transfer (such as Wi-Fi Aware or Wi-Fi Direct) only when needed.

    2. Eventual Consistency & Last-Write-Wins (LWW)

    In an off-grid mesh, devices are separated by distance and time. Device A and Device B might both update the status of the same survivor while disconnected from each other: - AirDB tracks edits using Hybrid Logical Clocks (HLC). Each edit receives an unambiguous timestamp that combines physical time, a counter, and a unique node identifier. - When Device A and Device B meet later, the update with the higher timestamp cleanly wins. - Deleted records are marked with tombstones so that older updates from stale devices cannot accidentally bring deleted rows back to life.

    3. Your Database Stays Yours (BYOD)

    AirDB does not force you to rewrite your entire database layer. You can use our ready-to-go SQLite adapter, or write a thin wrapper around your existing Room, CoreData, or GRDB schema. AirDB only needs to know how to read new changes and apply incoming updates.

    Required Permissions

    Android

    Your app will need standard Bluetooth permissions in AndroidManifest.xml: 

    <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.BLUETOOTH_SCAN" />
<uses-permission android:name="android.permission.BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE" />
<uses-permission android:name="android.permission.BLUETOOTH_CONNECT" />
<uses-permission android:name="android.permission.ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION" android:maxSdkVersion="30" />

    Apple (iOS / macOS)

    In your app's Info.plist, include a description explaining why your app uses Bluetooth: 

    <key>NSBluetoothAlwaysUsageDescription</key>
<string>AirDB uses Bluetooth to discover nearby devices and synchronize offline database records.</string>
